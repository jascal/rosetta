# Qwen3.5 singleton cross-context test

**Open:** do neurons603 and1765 explain routing transfers beyond their own
bisection discovery cases? Both individually sufficient interventions came from
the same three development vocabulary groups, not an independent test sample.

This study freezes three modes in all six directed contrasts: donor-gate changes
at neuron603, at neuron1765, and jointly. Eighteen forwards include exact repeats
of both discovered singleton substitutions. Their complete score hashes must
match the prior bisection outputs before interpreting effects. The previous
random two-neuron control transfers none and remains retained as a control on
these unchanged context pairs. Other MLP products remain the target's own.

All cases, candidate IDs, numeric payloads, source hashes and prior links are
frozen before new forwards. Datalog selects every site, verifies FP32-applied
values and computes all argmaxes from the full vocabulary. Reproduction failure
invalidates the effect interpretation. All failures are retained.

Shared sufficiency across these contrasts would motivate a common circuit
hypothesis, not establish generalization. Case-specific effects would reject a
simple shared-neuron explanation. Neither outcome supplies a token-only rule,
a fresh confirmation certificate, or positive incremental certified coverage.

## Completed outcome

**Empirical negative cross-context result:** all 18 forwards complete. Both
singleton discovery repeats reproduce their entire score hashes exactly. The
applied-patch summary passes. Neuron603 transfers one of six directions (its
original context0); neuron1765 transfers one (its original context54). Their
joint substitution transfers those same two contexts, with zero transfer in the
third group and zero reverse transfers. There is no newly rescued contrast.

Thus the two singleton interventions reproduce their discovery effects but do
not provide a shared explanation across the three groups. A singleton's
sufficiency must remain qualified by its context; neuron IDs alone do not define
a reusable copying rule. These failed cross-context tests remain retained.

The next extraction hypothesis should model source-choice computation using
token-computable features and source-value preferences, rather than equate a
shared neuron identity with the algorithm. That hypothesis must still specify
its build-time evidence, runtime rule and abstention guard, then freeze before
fresh disjoint confirmation. No such rule or certificate is claimed here.
